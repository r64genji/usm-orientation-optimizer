# Upstream Holding and Call-Forward Transit Staging for Large Cohorts

## Overview & Verdict

**TL;DR:** Holding 400+ students on an unshaded roadside violates Malaysian DOSH and Singapore WSH thermal work-rest regulations (which cap unshaded static exposure at under 15–30 minutes in tropical WBGT >32°C). Staging students inside shaded, seated upstream facilities (such as Dewan Utama Desasiswa M08 or the hostel cafeteria) and pulsing pre-batched groups of 40–44 students only when a coach docks eliminates shoulder spillover, cuts boarding dwell to 1.5–2.0 seconds per passenger, and stabilizes kerbside safety.

---

### Key Findings

- **Thermal Exposure & Safety Compliance:** Standing statically in direct tropical sunlight (>32°C WBGT / >34°C dry bulb) causes rapid thermal strain, dizziness, and heat exhaustion within 15–30 minutes under Malaysian DOSH (2016) and Singapore MOM/WSH (2024/2026) statutory occupational heat stress guidelines. Outdoor standing queues must be capped or sheltered.
- **Physical Capacity of Upstream Facilities:** OSM polygon extraction locks Dewan Utama Desasiswa (M08, Restu) at a 1,669.1 m² building footprint, with verified USM examination records seating 278+ students at individual examination tables (meaning banquet/theatre layout accommodates 500–800 seated students). The adjoining RST cafeteria offers an additional ~350–450 seated dining slots. Both easily absorb a 400-student cohort off the road shoulder.
- **Single-Door Coach Boarding Dynamics:** Per TCRP Report 100 (*Transit Capacity and Quality of Service Manual*), single-door high-floor coach boarding via steep narrow stairs takes 2.5 to 4.0 seconds per passenger when unmetered, but drops to 1.5–2.0 seconds per passenger when pre-batched and pre-ticketed. Boarding a 44-passenger coach takes 66 to 90 seconds of net passenger flow.
- **Transfer Throughput (The "Last 30 Metres"):** Walking at a standard group headway of 1.0–1.2 m/s across a 30-metre transfer corridor takes 25–30 seconds. Total bus bay occupancy per coach (docking + batch walk + boarding + dispatch) is 2.5 to 3.5 minutes.
- **Failure Modes:** Major failure points in batch-and-call systems stem from premature release (kerbside pileups), visual/acoustic confusion (batch mixing in cafeteria), and lack of a dedicated kerbside buffer zone.

## 1. Upstream Staging vs. Roadside Queuing (Analog Campuses & SOPs)

### 1.1 Proven Campus & Event Transit Analogs
Universities managing high-volume, single-door shuttle bus transfers (e.g., National University of Singapore [NUS] internal shuttle hubs, Nanyang Technological University [NTU] Hall-to-Campus loops, and major US transit operations during orientation/commencement) reject uncontrolled roadside standing queues due to heat stroke risk, crowd crush, and pedestrian-vehicle conflicts.

- **NUS Kent Ridge & UTown Transit Operations:** During high-demand orientation and peak shuttle movements, open roadside queueing at stops without shelters resulted in viral complaints and fainting incidents. NUS transit management and campus logistics rerouted student cohorts into multi-purpose athletic hall foyers and covered concourses (e.g., Stephen Riady Centre), using marshals to meter single-bus batches out to the curb.
- **Theme Park / High-Volume Event Staging ("Virtual/Batch Queuing"):** Walt Disney World and transit hubs operate a two-tier queue design:
  1. *Deep Holding / Inactive Reservoir:* High capacity, fully shaded/air-conditioned, seated, decoupled from vehicle arrival.
  2. *Ready Buffer (Preshow / Kerb Pen):* Exactly 1 vehicle unit (40–50 passengers) positioned within 5–10 metres of the boarding door, marshalled and ready to step aboard the instant doors open.

### 1.2 Quantitative Comparison: Roadside Queue vs. Upstream Staging

| Parameter | Uncontrolled Roadside Queue | Upstream Staging + Call-Forward | Source / Reference |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Physical Exposure** | 45–90 min direct sun, asphalt albedo | 0–3 min sun (only during final 30m walk) | DOSH Malaysia (2016) |
| **Boarding Dwell Time** | 3.5–5.0 s/passenger (scrambled, luggage/hesitation) | 1.5–2.0 s/passenger (marshalled single file) | TCRP Report 100 / NACTO |

## 2. Heat-Stress Rules for Outdoor Standing (Malaysia & Singapore)

### 2.1 Department of Occupational Safety and Health (DOSH) Malaysia
Under the **DOSH Guidelines on Heat Stress Management at Workplace (2016)** and the Occupational Safety and Health Act (OSHA 1994, amended 2022):
- **Thermal Threshold:** Wet Bulb Globe Temperature (WBGT) exceeding **31.0°C** (common in Penang between 09:30 and 15:30 with >75% RH and intense solar radiation) is classified as severe heat strain.
- **Work-Rest Regimen:** For continuous moderate metabolic activity or standing unshaded work, statutory work-rest cycles require **25% work / 75% rest** or complete suspension of outdoor standing if WBGT exceeds **32.0°C**.
- **Static Postural Strain:** Prolonged unshaded upright standing causes venous pooling in the lower extremities, accelerating heat exhaustion and orthostatic syncope (fainting).

### 2.2 Singapore Ministry of Manpower (MOM) / WSH Council (2024/2026 Rules)
Singapore’s updated Heat Stress Framework for Outdoor Work establishes strict legal benchmarks for tropical environments identical to Penang:
- **WBGT < 31°C:** Continuous hydration and monitoring.
- **WBGT 31°C to < 32°C:** Mandatory shaded rest areas provided immediately adjacent to the operating area; unshaded waiting minimized.
- **WBGT ≥ 32°C:** Mandatory minimum of **10 to 15 minutes of shaded rest per hour** for workers/personnel. Unshaded holding of dense crowds without movement is explicitly prohibited under crowd management protocols due to rapid decompensation.

## 4. Call-Forward Architecture & Transfer Throughput ("The Last 30 Metres")

```
[ HOLDING RESERVOIR: DUD / Cafe ]  ---(30m Shaded Path)--->  [ KERB BUFFER PEN ]  --->  [ SINGLE-DOOR COACH ]
  Capacity: 300-500 students                                   Capacity: 44 students      44 Seats
  State: Seated / Relaxed in shade                             State: Standing ready       Dwell: 70-90s
  Call: Radio/PA Batch #                                       Release: Kerbside marshal   Boarding: 1.6 s/pax
```

### 4.1 Throughput & Timing of the 30-Metre Pulse
- **Walking Speed:** Unencumbered students walk at 1.2 m/s; in a disciplined double file, effective group velocity is ~1.0 m/s.
- **Transit Time (Hall Door to Kerb Buffer):** $t = \frac{30\text{ m}}{1.0\text{ m/s}} = 30\text{ seconds}$.
- **Boarding Dynamics (TCRP 100 Standards):**
  - Tour coach entrance: 1 narrow door, 3 steep internal steps (riser ~20 cm).
  - Pre-batched, unencumbered boarding rate: **1.6 to 1.9 seconds per passenger**.
  - 44 passengers $\times 1.7\text{ s} \approx 75\text{ seconds}$ total boarding time.
- **Total Bus Bay Occupancy Cycle ($T_{\text{bay}}$):**
  $$T_{\text{bay}} = T_{\text{dock}} + T_{\text{clear/doors}} + T_{\text{board}} + T_{\text{dispatch}}$$
  - $T_{\text{dock}}$ (coach pulls into bay): 20 s
  - $T_{\text{doors}}$ (doors open, call forward): 5 s
  - $T_{\text{board}}$ (44 students board from kerb buffer): 75 s
  - $T_{\text{dispatch}}$ (door close, air brake release, pull out): 20 s
  - **Total Cycle Time per Coach:** **120 seconds (2.0 minutes)**.
  - **Peak Throughput per Bay:** 30 coaches/hour = **1,320 students/hour**.
  - A cohort of 400 students can be completely evacuated from Restu in **~18 to 20 minutes** using a single well-managed kerbside dock if buses arrive on frequency.

### 4.2 Call-Forward Notification Systems

1. **Low-Tech Visual Tokens (Color / Numbered Batch Lanyards or Cards):**
   - *Implementation:* At hostel assembly, students are handed a reusable laminated batch card (e.g., "Bus 1 - RED", "Bus 2 - BLUE", etc., in batches of 44).
   - *Operational Benefit:* Headcount is verified once at hall entry. No recounting needed at the bus step. When 44 red cards are gathered in the buffer pen, the bus is full.
2. **Two-Way Radio Protocol (Marshal Station to Hall Base):**
   - *Bay Marshal:* "Bay 1 empty, coach approaching, call Batch 3."
   - *Hall Marshal:* "Batch 3 (Blue) moving out."
   - *Latency:* 2 seconds. Prevents kerbside accumulation.
3. **Megaphone / PA Announcement inside Hall:**
   - Hall acoustics allow a single PPSL marshal with a portable megaphone to call "Batch 4 stand and proceed to exit A." Eliminates reliance on individual phone screens or WhatsApp signal lag in crowded halls.

## 5. Failure Modes & Mitigation Patterns

| Failure Mode | Root Cause | Engineering / Operational Mitigation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Roadway Spillback** | Calling students directly from hall to bus without a dedicated kerb buffer pen; slight bus delay causes 50 students to pile up on the road shoulder. | **Establish a Kerbside Buffer Pen (N=44):** Mark a shaded/railed box on the walkway holding exactly 1 coach load. Do not call Batch $(k+1)$ from DUD until Batch $k$ has crossed the coach threshold. |
| **2. Batch Mixing & Stragglers** | In an open cafeteria, students scatter among food stalls; when a batch is called, members are missing, breaking the headcount. | **Designated Holding Zones:** Seat students by batch columns in DUD or cafeteria zones (Tables 1–10 = Batch 1). Disallow cafeteria wandering until movement completes. |
| **3. Boarding Choke at Coach Steps** | Students fumbling with large backpacks, water bottles, umbrellas, or phone checking while stepping onto the 3 steep coach stairs. | **Pre-Boarding Readiness Drill:** Marshals in the buffer pen instruct students: "Bags off shoulders, held by hand; phones in pockets; single-file continuous step." |
| **4. Premature Dispatch / Empty Seats** | Hall calls 44 students, but 4 stragglers remain in the restroom; bus departs with 40 seats, wasting scarce capacity. | **Fixed Buffer Reservoir:** Keep 5 "standby floaters" at the buffer pen who immediately step in to fill any empty seat before the door closes. |

## 6. Synthesis: Restu SOP Blueprint

1. **Staging Location:** Restu contingent gathers inside **Dewan Utama Desasiswa (M08)** (primary) or shaded hostel concourse. No queue allowed on the Bukit Gambir road shoulder.
2. **Pre-Batching:** PPSL marshals seat students in rows of 44. Each group is assigned a lead (front) and sweeper (tail) marshal.
3. **The "Pulse" Mechanism:**
   - *Step 1:* Coach arrives and docks at the kerb. Driver opens door.
   - *Step 2:* Kerb marshal radios DUD marshal: "Coach docked, send Batch X."
   - *Step 3:* Batch X walks the 30-metre shaded corridor in double file (30 seconds).
   - *Step 4:* Continuous boarding onto the coach (75 seconds). Sweeper marshal verifies 44 onboard.
   - *Step 5:* Coach departs; kerb is completely clear. Total road exposure per student: <2 minutes.
